## Don't just build. Ship.

| | |

| **Who** | Anyone who wants to build — tech, business, design |
| **When** | Kickoff and build weekend 23–25 October · ship focus 26–28 October · pitch night 30 October |
| **Where** | Sydney · venue TBC |
| **Judged on** | A live demonstration, a commercial case, and verified signups |

HarbourHack is a hackathon where the demo is only a third of the score. Teams build across the opening weekend, then spend three scheduled days — Monday to Wednesday, no workshops, no sessions — getting real people to sign up, and come back on 30 October to report what happened.

**We'd like Brainfish teaching one of the weekend sessions** — the literacy layer under a room that can already build.

---

## 01 · Why Brainfish, specifically

### They can build now. They can't operate.

AI collapsed the build. Someone in their first year can ship a working product over a weekend, and that is no longer the interesting constraint — which is why only one of the three things we score is whether the thing runs.

What replaced it is literacy, and not the kind that means *can you drive the tool*. Most of this room can drive the tool. It means: has anyone ever shown you what a go-to-market or ops function actually does all day, which parts of it are now worth automating, and how to tell whether what the automation produced is good enough to put in front of a customer. Nobody teaches that. It isn't in a degree, and you don't pick it up from a job you haven't had yet.

That is the gap you said Brainfish wants to invest in, and it's the gap this event walks teams into on day two — the moment they have something that runs and have to go and find a person who wants it.

HarbourHack is scored on three things, weighted equally:

| | Criterion | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Live demonstration** | The product runs, in front of the room, without a video. |
| 02 | **Commercial case** | Who it's for, what they do today, and why they'd switch. |
| 03 | **Verified signups** | Real users who opted in during the event. Not your group chat. |

Two of those three have nothing to do with code, and they are exactly where a team without that literacy stalls.

There's a symmetry worth naming. Brainfish's thesis is that a complex product is only as good as the knowledge layer that makes it understandable. HarbourHack's is that a built product is only as good as the person who can say who it's for. It's the same problem, one level up — and it's why we came to you for teaching rather than for tooling.

We're not proposing Brainfish as a build-stack sponsor competing for the same slot as everyone else. In a room this size, teaching is the highest-leverage thing a company can do, and it's the one thing a logo cannot buy.

## 02 · The format, and why it's shaped like this

Seven days, three of them for selling.

| | Date | |
|---|---|---|
| **Day one** | Friday 23 October | **Kickoff.** Teams form. Scope narrows. Workshop: cold outreach that gets replies. |
| **Days two–three** | Sat 24 – Sun 25 October | **Build focus.** Workshops run — the proposed Brainfish slot sits here. Mentors on the floor. Ship v1 and begin outreach. |
| **Days four–six** | Mon 26 – Wed 28 October | **Ship focus.** No workshops, no sessions. Chase signups, act on what comes back. |
| | *Thursday 29 October* | off |
| **Day seven** | Friday 30 October | **Pitch night.** Live demonstrations and judging. |

The back half is the whole point. A team that has only ever shown its product to a judging panel hasn't shipped anything — it has performed. Most hackathons leave the selling to whoever still has the energy on Monday; here it's three days of the programme.

That matters for a teaching partner more than it might look. A workshop on the Saturday or Sunday isn't the end of the encounter: whatever a team walks out with, they then apply unsupervised for three days under real pressure, and report the result in front of the room on the Friday. Very few workshops get graded a week later.

Session times are TBC.

## 03 · The ask

Three things, and a fourth if it suits. The workshop is the one that matters most — the other two are what turn a good session into something that outlives the weekend.

### 01 · One weekend workshop slot — the one you offered to consider

Saturday 24 or Sunday 25 October, taught by your team. Length TBC; our instinct is ninety minutes with hands on keyboards rather than a keynote.

Aimed squarely at people who have never worked inside a GTM or ops function. What we'd want it to leave behind isn't a product walkthrough — it's a working mental model: what these functions actually do all day, which parts are now automatable and which only look like they are, and how you check whether what came out is something you'd be willing to send to a customer.

If the new product you walked us through is at a stage where you'd want people inside it, the workshop is the natural place to put them there. Our only ask is that the session still stands up for a team that never touches it again — that's what makes it teaching rather than a demo, and it's what makes it worth publishing afterwards.

### 02 · Tooling access for attendees, for a month

Access for everyone who attends, running from the kickoff on 23 October to 23 November — a month, rather than the eight days of the event.

The event window is the wrong length for what we're asking you to teach. The session lands on the Saturday; the three scored days test whether it stuck; and then the teams that got somewhere keep going, which is where a habit either forms or doesn't. A month is long enough for someone to use the thing on a problem we didn't set them.

Happy to size this once registrations give us a real number. We'd rather tell you how many people than guess at it now.

### 03 · $2,500 toward the run cost

Not a naming-rights figure and not a tier — we don't have tiers, and what's on the table is still open. It goes to the things that decide whether a weekend is worth attending rather than enduring: the room, the food, the printing.

We'd rather name a number than send you a menu. If it's the wrong number for what you're getting back, say so and we'll talk about what the right one is.

### 04 · Optional, if it suits

- **Someone on the floor** — an hour or two of office hours on the Saturday, or an operator walking the floor while teams make decisions. The cheapest thing on this list and, every time we've seen it, the thing teams remember.
- **A seat on the judging panel** at pitch night, 30 October.
- **A prize category**, if there's one you'd want your name against. We'd rather you chose it than we invented one for you.

## 04 · Straight about the stage we're at

This is the first HarbourHack.

We're not going to quote you an attendance number, a reach figure or an impressions estimate, because every one of those would be invented and you'd be right not to believe them. That matters more than usual here: the return you're after is brand and community, which is exactly the kind of number that is easiest to make up and hardest to check.

What we can tell you is what's fixed and what isn't. The dates are locked. The format is locked. The judging criteria are locked. Venue, prizes, mentors, the partner roster and the shape of pitch night are all still open — which is the useful part of coming in now. A partner who arrives at this stage helps decide what the event is, rather than getting a logo on something already built.

If audience numbers matter to your decision — and they reasonably might — we'd rather walk you through the projection and the reasoning behind it than put a confident figure in a PDF.

## 05 · The return

**Grassroots literacy, in the room where it's scarcest.** Every team in the building, for the length of one session, taught by people who build the thing rather than by someone reading from a deck. Not a stand, not a stall — the floor.

**A Sydney company teaching the Sydney room.** Nearly everything these teams will be taught about AI this year was built somewhere else by someone who will never be in the room with them. Brainfish was founded here. That's a different kind of credibility, and it doesn't transfer to the companies that fly in.

**Thought leadership that's demonstrated rather than claimed.** A session that measurably upskills a room is a stronger artefact than a panel seat, and it's yours to publish — recording, slides, write-up, whatever you want out of it. We'll help produce it and we'll carry it.

**A look at early-career people actually working.** The complaint about junior hires is always the same: they can build, but they can't find the customer, write the email or hold the conversation. Those are the two criteria this event scores hardest. You'd be watching the exact thing you interview for and rarely get to observe.

**The new product in front of its future operators.** Not its buyers — the people who will be running these functions in three years and are meeting the category for the first time.

**Partner billing** on the site, the registration flow and event collateral, and a seat in the room on pitch night, 30 October.
